Grispy — Licence terms

Effective 3 September 2026. Applies to the Grispy browser extension for Chrome and Firefox.

Grispy is free for personal use, and stays free. Every form, every saved answer, no limit and no account. Using Grispy as part of your work needs a paid licence — that is the only thing money changes here.

What free means

Free is the whole product, not a trial of it. It does not expire, it does not count your forms, and it does not ask you to upgrade to finish something you started. If Grispy can fill a form, the free version fills it.

Personal use means use that is neither part of paid work nor on behalf of a business. Filling in your own tax declaration, your family's forms, a school application, an insurance renewal — all personal, all free, however often you do it.

When you need a licence

A paid licence is required for any other use. In practice that means:

  • using Grispy in a job you are paid for;
  • using it on behalf of a business — your own, your employer's, or a client's.

It does not matter whether Grispy is the main tool or an occasional one, and it does not matter who is being paid. If the typing is work, the licence applies.

The two paid licences

ProCovers one named person. Suits a professional working alone.
TeamCovers the number of people stated on your invoice, with a minimum of three. Suits an office where several people fill the same portals.

Both run for the period carried inside the licence key, and both are renewed by entering a new key. A licence is granted to people, not to computers — one person may use their licence on the machines they work on.

The first seven days

Paid features work for seven days after you install Grispy, without a licence and without giving us anything. After that they stop until a key is entered. The rest of the extension carries on exactly as before — nothing you saved during those seven days is taken away, and nothing you can do on day one becomes impossible on day eight.

What no licence includes

Free or paid, a licence lets you run Grispy. It does not let you redistribute it, resell it, or publish a modified version of it. Grispy is proprietary software; the source is not public.

What paying does not change

Grispy makes no network requests of any kind, on any tier. A licence key is checked on your own computer against a key built into the extension — nothing is sent anywhere, and there is no account, no login and no server that knows you exist. The privacy policy is the same document for a paying customer as for anyone else, and it is written so you can verify it rather than trust it.

The one consequence worth stating plainly: because we cannot see your installation, we also cannot recover anything from it. That is unchanged by paying, and the support page explains what it means in practice.

Changes to these terms

If these terms change, the effective date above changes with them. A change that narrows what a licence covers applies from the next renewal, never retroactively to a licence already bought.
