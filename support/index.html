Grispy — Support

Bugs, questions and ideas. One address, and a plain account of what to expect.

Write to dchrysomallis@outlook.com. Grispy is free, there is no separate support product, and it is written by one person. So this is best effort: no help desk, no ticket number, no promised response time. We would rather say that than imply a service level that does not exist.

What to expect

Email is the only channel. There is no chat widget, no contact form and no forum — the same reason there is no analytics: this site makes no network requests of its own, and a form would have to send your message through somebody else's server before it reached us. A mailto: link sends it straight from your mail client to us, and nowhere else.

Bugs that stop Grispy working are answered first. Everything else — questions, ideas, "why does it do that" — is answered when there is time, and sometimes the answer is no.

Two things worth checking before you write

Both are limits of the design rather than faults, and neither can be undone from our end, because nothing you save in Grispy ever reaches us:

  • A forgotten passphrase cannot be recovered. Not by you, and not by us. If you have a readable export taken while unlocked, use it; otherwise resetting Grispy is the only way forward, and it deletes what is stored. The privacy policy explains why — the key exists only in your browser's memory, for one session, and is never written down anywhere.
  • Deleting is immediate and final. There is no copy on a server to restore from, because there is no server. Export before you reset Grispy or uninstall it.

Reporting a bug

Grispy has no crash reporting and no telemetry, so a fault on your computer tells us nothing at all unless you write. That also means the description in your email is the only evidence there is. What helps:

Browser and versionChrome or Firefox, and the version number from its About page.
Grispy's versionShown on the extension's options page.
The formThe address of the page it is on, if that page is public. A form behind a login we cannot reach is still worth reporting — say so, and describe it instead.
What you didThe clicks, in order, from opening Grispy to the point it went wrong.
What happenedWhat you expected, and what happened instead. A screenshot of the form, with your own answers removed, is usually worth more than a paragraph.

Please do not send us your saved data. Not an export file, not a screenshot of filled-in fields. Those hold whatever you typed into the form, which is exactly the material Grispy is built never to transmit — and an email would carry it out of your control and into ours. We do not want a copy, and we do not need one to fix a bug.

Suggesting a change

The most useful thing you can send is a form Grispy handles badly: a wizard it loses track of between steps, a field it will not fill, a control it does not recognise. A public URL for one of those is worth more than a feature description, because it can be tested against directly.

Feature requests are welcome and are read. Grispy is deliberately small, and staying small is part of how it keeps its permission list to three lines, so a good suggestion may still be declined on those grounds. If it is, you will be told why.

Once Grispy is published

Grispy is not yet on the Chrome Web Store or addons.mozilla.org. When it is, its listings will point their support link here, and the store review pages become a second place to raise something in public. This address stays the direct route either way.

Privacy enquiries

Questions about what Grispy stores, or about data protection, go to the same address and are covered by the privacy policy, which names it as the contact for exactly that purpose.
