The Settings page

Everything Grispy has saved, in one list — and the only place that can delete any of it, back it up, or restore it.

Open it from the gear in the top-right of the popup. It opens in its own tab. The line under the heading totals what is stored — something like 3 forms · 7 presets · 0 unreachable records — and below it are four tabs.

Saved forms

Every form Grispy holds anything for, each one expandable. A form's summary line reads 2 steps · 3 presets · last saved 14/08/2026, and opening it shows the pages that form covers and the presets saved against it.

Step 1 — /apply/details One page of the form. Forms that span several pages list each; a single-page form shows Part of a saved form instead.
Delete a preset Removes one set of answers from this form. The form and its other presets stay.
Export this form Writes a file holding just this form. Importing it elsewhere adds that form rather than replacing everything — see Backup below.
Delete this form Removes the form, all its presets and all its page entries.
Nothing is saved yet. What the tab shows before you have saved anything.

Every deletion asks first, naming exactly what goes — Delete the preset "Default" of /apply/details? This cannot be undone. — and none of them can be undone.

Unreachable records

A section that appears only when there is something in it. These are records nothing on any page can reach any more: leftovers from a form that changed its address, or from a page you detached from a form. Each is described and can be removed individually.

One case it flags deserves care rather than a click. A form whose saved presets match no field on its page may instead be two forms sharing one address — splitting that is the step controls' job in the popup, not this page's. Deleting it here throws away answers that a split would have recovered.

Encryption

Setting, changing, or turning off the passphrase, and locking the store early. All of it is covered in protecting your saved data.

Backup

Grispy holds nothing on a server, so nothing follows you to a second computer on its own. A file is how data moves — to another machine, to another browser, or to somewhere safe before you do something irreversible.

Export everything

Writes a file named grispy-export-2026-09-01.json. Grispy asks which kind you want, and the choice matters:

Readable copy Plain text, openable by anything. This is the only way back into your data if you forget your passphrase — and it holds everything you ever saved, in the clear, so where you keep it matters as much as the passphrase does.
Encrypted Asks for a passphrase for the file itself. That passphrase is what restores it — it can differ from the browser's, and the file will need whichever one you typed here.

Export needs the store unlocked. While locked, Grispy says Unlock first — a locked store has nothing readable to export. rather than writing a file that looks like a backup and restores nothing.

Import a file

Grispy reads the file, works out which kind it is, and tells you exactly what will happen before anything is written:

A full backup Replaces everything. This is a full backup: 3 forms and 7 presets. Everything currently saved — 2 forms — will be deleted and replaced. It replaces rather than merges because two machines can hold different forms at the same address, and merging them would produce a form that is neither.
A single form Adds it. This file holds one form: 1 form and 2 presets. Your other forms are untouched. If it claims a page you have already saved, the message names what will be replaced.

The confirmation offers "Export first" when the import would destroy something — and only then. A restore cannot be undone, and the moment before one is when a backup is worth most. It is an offer, not an obstacle; you can decline it.

Danger zone

One button: Clear everything. It asks first, naming the count — Delete everything Grispy has saved — 47 records? This cannot be undone.

It deletes every form, preset and pointer, and the vault key with them. It has its own tab rather than sitting at the foot of the list for the obvious reason, and it is deliberately available while locked, because of the less obvious one: it is the only way back in if you forget your passphrase. Resetting works when unlocking cannot.

Known limits

  • Every deletion here is immediate and final. There is no copy on a server to restore from, because there is no server. Export before you clear anything or uninstall.
  • Import replaces or adds; it never merges. A full backup takes the place of what you have.
  • Uninstalling the extension deletes its storage, the same as clearing it.
  • A form is listed under the path of whichever of its pages was saved first, so a multi-step form is named after one arbitrary step of itself. Readable form names are not built yet.

The footer

Below the tabs, on every one of them: a link to the privacy policy, the support address, and the build version — Grispy 0.1.0. The version is there because a bug report that does not say which build it came from costs a round trip to establish it. What else helps in a report.
