What a fill reports

What Grispy tells you before a fill, what it marks on the page during one, and what the message afterwards counts — including the one thing it deliberately does not say.

Press Fill and a short message appears at the bottom of the popup. It is not decoration. Grispy checks every field after writing to it, and that message is the result of the check — which is why the number it reports is sometimes lower than the number of fields you can see filled in on the page.

Three things report a fill, and the message is only the last of them: a line before you press Fill saying what it will write, a mark on each field it touches while it writes, and the message afterwards. This page covers all three.

Before: the line above the buttons

When a set of details is selected, a line above the buttons says what Fill is about to do — Fill writes 6 of 14 fields. — and names the form's own preset if that is the one not being applied. It is the only place anything tells you what a fill will do before it does it, and it is explained on that page. It does not appear when you are filling from a form's own saved answers.

During: the marks on the page

Grispy puts a chip beside every field it touched, on the page itself. The counts below cannot tell you which fields they mean; these can.

FILLEDWritten and read back successfully. The same fields the first number counts.
UNVERIFIEDWritten, or already correct, but Grispy could not prove it afterwards. The second number.
NOT FILLEDGrispy found the field and could not write it — a control that refused, or a value the page rewrote.

Three things to know about them:

  • A field Grispy could not find carries no chip at all. There is nothing on the page to put one beside. Those still count against the total in the message, which is why a missing chip and a Not filled chip are different answers.
  • They are drawn over the page, not into it. Nothing on the form is modified to show them, and nothing about them is submitted with it. They clear the moment you touch the page — your first real click or key press removes them all.
  • A chip means written, never overwritten. It does not say whether there was something in the field before. That matters if you fill twice — see the order that matters.

After: the message

The message, in full

At most three things appear, and only the first is always there:

Filled 7 of 10 fields. 2 could not be verified. · 3 of 5 saved rows present
Filled 7 of 10 fields. Always shown. 10 is how many fields Grispy had a saved answer for on this page. 7 is how many of those it wrote and then read back successfully.
2 could not be verified. Only when that number is above zero. Grispy wrote to the field, or found the right answer already in it, but could not prove it afterwards.
· 3 of 5 saved rows present Only on a form with repeating rows, and only when some are missing. Nothing to do with the field counts before it — see forms that repeat a set of fields.

What "10" counts, and what it does not

The second number is the answers this preset holds for this page — not the fields on the page. Those are different, and the difference is usually the explanation for a count that looks wrong.

  • A field you did not tick when you saved is not counted. Saving lets you choose which fields to keep, so a form with 30 fields where you saved 10 reports out of 10, not out of 30.
  • Fields belonging to another step are not counted. On a form that spans several pages, each page is filled from the answers saved for that page.
  • Nothing is counted twice. On a page built from frames, every frame is asked and the answers are merged, so a field living in a frame counts once.

The gap: what happened to the other 3

When the first number is lower than the second, one of three things happened to each missing field — and Grispy does not tell you which:

It was not there Nothing on the page matched the saved field. Common when a form changes, when a section is collapsed or hidden, or when a page renders fewer rows than you saved.
A control refused The field is driven by a search-as-you-type dropdown or tag picker, and the widget would not take the value. Grispy will not force a value past a control that owns it, because a value written past the control is one the page never registers.
It did not hold Grispy wrote the value, read the field back, and found something else there. Usually the page rejected or rewrote it.

This is a real limit and worth stating plainly: the three are counted the same way, so the message cannot distinguish "Grispy could not find this field" from "the page would not accept this value." What it will never do is count any of them as filled. A number in that first position has been read back from the control.

The chips on the page close part of that gap, if you look before you click anything: a field carrying NOT FILLED was found and refused, and a field with no chip was never found at all. What neither the chips nor the message separate is a control that refused the value from a page that rewrote it.

What to do about it

Look at the form. The fields Grispy could not fill are the ones still empty or still wrong, and there is no substitute for checking — which you should do anyway before submitting anything that matters. If a form fails the same way every time, it is worth reporting; a form Grispy handles badly is the single most useful thing you can send.

"Could not be verified" is not "failed"

This one confuses people, so it is worth being exact. An unverified field is one where Grispy did the work and the check came back inconclusive — not one where the work failed.

It happens with dropdowns and pickers that draw their own display over an ordinary form control. Grispy drives them the way you would, then reads what the widget is showing to confirm the value took. When a widget's display cannot be read at all, there is nothing to confirm against. The value is very probably in place; Grispy will not claim it is.

The alternative would be to count it as filled and be wrong some of the time, which is the failure this whole check exists to prevent. A form that reports success and submits the old value is worse than one that says it is not sure.

When the count is zero, or there is no message at all

No message appears Grispy had nothing saved for this page, so nothing was attempted. Check that the preset you meant is the one selected.
Filled 0 of N Every field was attempted and none could be confirmed. Usually the page changed, or this is a different step of the form from the one the answers were saved on.
Failed to fill form. Please try again. Grispy could not reach the page at all — it navigated, or closed, mid-fill. Nothing was written.

Known limits

  • The three failure kinds are not distinguished. As above. The count tells you how many need attention, not which kind of attention.
  • The check reads the control, not the page's mind. Grispy confirms the field holds the value. It cannot know whether the page's own validation is satisfied, so a form can fill correctly and still refuse to submit until you touch a field yourself.
  • Some fields are invisible to Grispy entirely — rich-text editors, fields built inside a web component's shadow DOM, and custom pickers with no ordinary form control underneath. These were never saved, so they are not in either number.
  • The message describes one fill. It is not stored, and pressing Fill again produces a fresh one.

Every message, in one place

Filled 7 of 10 fields. 7 fields written and confirmed, out of 10 Grispy had answers for.
2 could not be verified. 2 more were written, or already correct, but could not be confirmed.
· 3 of 5 saved rows present A repeating group is showing fewer rows than you saved. More.
Failed to fill form. Please try again. Grispy lost the page mid-fill. Nothing was written.
Fill writes 6 of 14 fields. Above the buttons, before you press Fill, when a set of details is selected.
FILLED · UNVERIFIED · NOT FILLED The chips on the page itself, beside each field Grispy touched. Cleared by your first click.
