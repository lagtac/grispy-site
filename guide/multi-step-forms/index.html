Forms that span several pages

Multi-step wizards: the line that says where Grispy thinks you are, how to correct it, and the two kinds of wizard it cannot follow.

Some forms are one page. Plenty are not — job applications, visa forms, checkouts and insurance quotes hand you a "Next" button and a fresh page for each part. Grispy can save all of those pages into one preset, so a single saved identity covers the whole application instead of leaving you with four unrelated fragments. It needs one small gesture from you the first time you visit each page, and never again afterwards.

The line that tells you where you are

Open the popup on any page and there is a small grey line just under the field count. It always says what Grispy currently believes this page is:

Step 2 of 3 This page is the second of three you have saved into one form.
Part of a saved form Grispy knows this page, and the form has just the one page.
Which step is this? Most of the saved answers are missing, and this page has never been named as a step of its own — so Grispy is no longer sure which page this is.
Not part of a saved form Grispy has never seen this address.

The line is also a button. Click it and a small window opens asking This page is — and whatever you answer there is what Grispy believes from then on. That is the whole mechanism: one line that states what it thinks, and one click to correct it.

The line stops being a button in exactly one case: when you have nothing saved anywhere at all. There is no form to attach a page to yet, so there is nothing to correct. Save a page first.

A page you have already named keeps its name. "Which step is this?" only comes up on pages Grispy has never been told about. Once you have said a page is a step of its own, it is filed under its own address and goes on reading "Step 2 of 3" even if the site later changes most of the boxes on it. You will see that as "· 2 fields changed" beside the save date, and Fill stays the main button. A site being updated is not the same thing as a page being mistaken for another one, and only the second is worth interrupting you over.

Telling Grispy this is a different page

You will see this on wizards where only a query or a # fragment changes: site.com/wizard?step=1 → ?step=2 → ?step=3, or site.com/app#/details → #/payment.

Grispy deliberately ignores the ?… and #… parts of an address when it files a page away — otherwise a stray tracking parameter would make the same form look like a brand new one every visit. The cost of that is that step 2 and step 3 of this kind of wizard can look identical to it.

Walkthrough

You are filling in a visa application at visa.example.com/wizard?step=1.

  1. Step 1. You fill it in and Save. Normal Grispy. The line reads "Part of a saved form".
  2. You click Next and land on ?step=2.
  3. Open the popup. Grispy thinks it knows this page — it shows your preset — but the saved answers are step 1's, and most of them are not on screen. So the line reads "Which step is this?", and Save takes over as the main button. Fill is still there beside it, but it is no longer what Grispy recommends: it would write step 1's answers into step 2's boxes.
  4. Click the line. The window opens:

    This page is

    ○ A new step

    ○ Not part of a form

  5. Choose "A new step" and click Make a new step. Grispy files this page as a step of its own, leaving step 1 exactly as it was. A toast confirms "Saved as a new step." The line now reads "Step 2 of 2".
  6. Fill and Save. Step 2's answers join step 1's inside the same preset.

Every later visit to ?step=2 lands on the right data with no message and nothing to click. A four-step wizard costs three corrections in total, ever.

If you skip a step

Steps you have never named still fall back to the first one you saved. So if you jump straight to ?step=9, the line asks "Which step is this?" again. Answering it fixes that page the same way it fixed the others. This is the same mechanism, not a fault.

When the address never changes, but the page still shows where you are

You will see this on wizards where clicking "Next" redraws the page in place — the address bar never moves, not even a ? or a # — but the page itself still shows something that says which step you are on: a highlighted item in a list of steps, a tab marked as the current one, or a heading like "Step 2: Payment".

This is one degree harder than the case above, because there is nothing in the address at all to go on. Grispy handles it the same way a person would: by reading what the page itself is telling you.

Walkthrough

You are filling in an application at example.com/apply, a single address for the whole thing.

  1. Step 1. You fill it in and Save. The line reads "Part of a saved form".
  2. You click Next. The address does not change, but the page redraws with step 2's boxes, and the step list on screen now highlights "Payment" instead of "Details".
  3. Open the popup. Most of what was saved is not on this page, so the line reads "Which step is this?", exactly as it would for an address-based wizard.
  4. Click the line. Because Grispy noticed what the page itself is showing, the window names it:

    This page is

    ○ A new step — "Payment"

    ○ Not part of a form

  5. Choose "A new step — 'Payment'" and confirm. A toast confirms "Saved as a new step." The line now reads "Step 2 of 2".
  6. Fill and Save. Step 2's answers join step 1's inside the same preset.

Every later visit to this page is recognised the same way — by what it shows, not by its address — with nothing to click.

When the page shows nothing to go on

If the window's option just reads "A new step" with no name in quotes, or is greyed out with a reason underneath, the page is not showing Grispy anything it can use — see Known limits.

Joining a page to a form you already saved

You will see this on addresses that change as you advance: site.com/apply → site.com/apply/experience → site.com/apply/references.

Here the second page has an address Grispy has never seen, so the line reads "Not part of a saved form".

  1. Page 1 (/apply) asks for your name, email and phone. You fill it in, click Save, and name the preset "Me".
  2. You click the site's Next button and land on /apply/experience.
  3. Open the popup, and click the line. A window opens listing every form you have ever saved — one row each:

    careers.example.com — Job application

    2 steps · saved 19/08/2026

  4. Click the row for the job application. The window closes and the popup redraws as though it had recognised the page all along: your "Me" chip is back and selected, and the line reads "Step 2 of 2".
  5. Fill the page in and Save. Your experience details are added to the same "Me" preset. Page 1's saved data is not touched.

Repeat once for page 3, page 4, and so on. Afterwards nothing changes: the next visit is recognised straight away and offers Fill.

Filling a form that spans several pages

Grispy fills one page at a time. There is no button that completes a whole four-page application in one go.

On each page of the wizard you open the popup and click Fill; that page's saved answers go in, you click the site's Next, and you repeat. The preset holds all four pages, but each page is filled on the visit where you are actually looking at it. What the fill message tells you is the same on every step.

This is deliberate. Grispy can only read and write the page currently in front of it — it never works on pages in the background.

Taking a page back out

Joined a page to the wrong form? Named a step by mistake?

Click the line and choose "Not part of a form", then Remove from this form. A toast confirms "Removed from the form." The page goes back to being unrecognised, with Save on screen exactly as before you touched it.

What happens to the answers you saved on that page

They are kept — the window says so as soon as you pick the row: "Saved fields are kept if you attach it again." Put the page back in the same form and everything you had saved there comes straight back.

Be aware of the in-between, though: while a page is out, the answers it held are still stored, but nothing will fill them until you put the page back. They are not lost, and they are not hidden either — the Settings page lists them under Unreachable records as "Data for a page detached from this form — kept so that reattaching it restores the data.", and offers to remove them if that is what you want. It never removes them on its own, precisely because putting the page back is meant to bring them back.

When removing is refused

Sometimes "Not part of a form" is greyed out, with the reason underneath: "Remove /wizard.html?step=3 from this form first."

This happens on the first page of a same-address wizard — the ?step=1 page above — once you have named later steps off it. That first page is what the later ones are found through; removing it would strand them, and you would end up with a second, duplicate copy of the form.

Take the later steps out first, then that one. Or simply leave it: nothing is wrong with the form as it stands.

If that leaves nothing you can choose at all, the window still opens and still tells you why — it just offers Close rather than a button that would do nothing.

When Grispy asks before saving

If most of a page's saved answers have gone missing and its address is one Grispy cannot tell from a page you already saved, then pressing Save does not write straight away. It asks first.

The reason is that saving replaces everything stored for that page. If Grispy has the wrong page in mind, saving would quietly destroy another step's answers with nothing to undo it. So it puts the same This page is window in front of you and names what it would overwrite:

  • Replace Step 2 — yes, this really is step 2, my answers just changed.
  • Save as a new step — no, this is a different page; give it its own slot.
  • Cancel (or Esc) — neither; take me back. Your tick-boxes are exactly as you left them.

This only happens when most of the saved answers are missing. A form that renames one box out of seven is the site being updated, not a page being mistaken for another, and Grispy saves that without a word.

When two pages look identical

Sometimes there is nothing in the address to separate two pages at all. Then the window carries a note instead of a fix:

"This page's address is identical to the saved step's, so Grispy cannot tell them apart."

There is no "A new step" to choose, because there is nothing Grispy could file the page under. If you are saving, you can still confirm the overwrite — that choice is yours to make and the window makes sure it is deliberate. See Known limits.

Known limits

A wizard that shows nothing to tell its steps apart

A few sites are built so that clicking "Next" swaps the page's contents without changing the address and without showing anything on screen that says which step you are on — no highlighted item in a step list, no selected tab, no heading like "Step 2: Payment". Grispy has no way to tell those steps apart, so it treats them all as one page.

You will recognise one by the note above — the addresses are identical and there is no new step to make — or, in the window, by an "A new step" option with no name in quotes, greyed out with the reason "Nothing on this page tells it apart from the saved step." That is a known gap, not a bug: there is nothing on either the address or the page for Grispy to key a new step by. What protects you meanwhile is the question in When Grispy asks before saving — you will not overwrite a step silently.

That same greyed option appears in a second situation, and it is worth knowing them apart even though the wording is the same. Some wizards do mark their steps, but mark two of them identically — the same tab name, or the same heading, on what are really two different steps. Grispy will already have matched this page to the step saved under that marker, so making a new step here would give you two steps wearing one label and no way to tell which is which afterwards. It refuses for the same underlying reason: whatever the page shows, it does not tell this page apart from the one already saved. If you know the two steps are genuinely different, the addresses are what would separate them — a wizard that puts anything at all in its address, even ?step=2, is one Grispy can split.

A wizard that hides its old step instead of swapping it out

Some wizards keep every step's boxes present on the page the whole time and only hide the ones you are not currently on, rather than removing them when you click "Next". Grispy reads every box on the page whether or not it is hidden — so on a wizard built this way, it always sees all of every step's boxes at once, and always believes whatever you saved is still a complete match for the page, no matter which step you have actually clicked through to.

You will recognise this one because the line never says "Which step is this?" at all, even once you can plainly see you have moved to a different step — it just keeps reading whatever it read the first time, such as "Part of a saved form". This is a limitation of how that particular wizard is built, not a mistake in what you saved, and there is no click-through fix for it today. Fixing it means teaching Grispy to notice which boxes on a page are actually visible, which is separate, forward-looking work — not this feature, which only reads what the page reports.
