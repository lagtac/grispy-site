Grispy — Privacy Policy

Effective 28 August 2026. Applies to the Grispy browser extension for Chrome and Firefox.

Grispy sends nothing anywhere. It has no server, no account, no analytics and no tracking. Everything you save stays in your own browser, on your own computer, and can be encrypted with a passphrase only you know. We never receive it, so there is nothing for us to sell, lose or hand over.

1. What Grispy stores

When you save a form, Grispy records it on your device so it can fill the same form again later:

  • The form's structure — its field labels, names, types and the options in its dropdowns.
  • The address of each page the form spans (its origin and path; the query string and fragment are discarded).
  • The values you saved against that form, under whatever profile name you gave them.

Those values are whatever you chose to put in the form. That may include personal information — your name, address, telephone number, or anything else you typed. Grispy does not inspect it, categorise it, or treat one kind differently from another.

2. Where it is stored, and who can reach it

All of it is written to your browser's local extension storage (chrome.storage.local), which is a file inside your browser profile directory on your own computer. Grispy never uses your browser's sync storage, so nothing is copied to your other devices or to your browser vendor's servers.

Encryption, and why it is your choice

Grispy can encrypt what it saves — every value you saved, and the names you gave your forms and profiles — with a passphrase only you know. The first time you save anything, Grispy asks whether you want one. You can decline, and it will not ask again; you can turn encryption on later from its options page whenever you change your mind.

When encryption is on, you enter your passphrase once per browser session. It never leaves your computer and is never sent anywhere. The key it produces is held in your browser's memory for that session alone — it is never written to disk — and it is gone once you close the browser.

Four limits you should know about, stated plainly:

  • If you decline the passphrase, nothing is encrypted. Your saved data is stored as ordinary readable text, and anything that can read files on your computer — other software running under your user account, another person on the same unlocked machine, or someone who takes an unencrypted laptop — can read it. Your operating system's full-disk encryption and a locked screen are then what protect it.
  • Encryption conceals what you saved, not which sites you saved it on. Grispy has to know whether a page has anything saved for it without asking for your passphrase on every page you visit — otherwise you would be prompted constantly, nearly always on pages with nothing saved. The list of addresses it holds data for is therefore left readable. Someone with access to the files on your computer could see which sites you have saved forms for, and how many forms and profiles you have, without being able to read any of the values inside them.
  • A forgotten passphrase cannot be recovered. Not by you, and not by us — we never have it. Your options at that point are an export you took while unlocked, or resetting Grispy, which deletes everything and starts you over. Resetting works even when you cannot unlock.
  • Uninstalling deletes it. Removing the extension removes its storage. Export your data first if you want to keep it.

3. What Grispy does not do

Sends your data to us or anyone elseNo. The extension makes no network requests of any kind.
Analytics, telemetry or crash reportingNone. We collect no usage data, not even anonymous counts.
Accounts or sign-inNone. Grispy has no user accounts.
Advertising, tracking or profilingNone, and no data is ever sold, rented or shared.
Third-party libraries or SDKsNone. No package is installed and no external code is loaded. The one piece of third-party material bundled is the path data that draws the icons, under open-source licences named in the extension's third-party notice; nothing is fetched at runtime.
Cookies, browsing history, downloadsNot read. Grispy holds no permission for any of them.
Reading pages in the backgroundNo. Grispy can only see a page after you click its toolbar button on that tab.

4. Permissions, and why each one exists

activeTabLets Grispy read the page in the one tab where you clicked its button, and only after you click. It expires. It grants no standing access to any site.
scriptingLets Grispy place its form-reading code into that same tab, at that same moment.
storageLets Grispy keep your saved forms on your device.

Grispy requests no host permissions. That is why installing it shows no "read and change your data on all websites" warning: it genuinely cannot, until you ask it to, on the one tab you asked it on.

5. How to check this for yourself

Three ways, none of which requires trusting this page:

  • Open your browser's extension settings and read Grispy's permission list. It is the three above. A permission the extension does not hold is a capability it does not have.
  • Unpack the extension and search its code for the ways a browser can reach the network — fetch, XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket, sendBeacon, EventSource. There are no matches, in any version we have published.
  • The encryption is your browser's own — the built-in WebCrypto implementation, AES-GCM for the saved data and PBKDF2 for the passphrase. Grispy ships no cryptographic code of its own, so there is no bespoke implementation of ours for you to have to trust.

6. Your control over your data

Grispy's options page lets you see everything it has stored, export it to a JSON file, and delete it — one profile, one form, or everything at once. When encryption is on you choose the export format: encrypted with your passphrase, or readable. A readable export is the only copy that survives a forgotten passphrase, so it is worth keeping one somewhere safe — and worth remembering that it is readable by anything that can open the file.

Deletion is immediate and final; there is no copy anywhere else to delete.

Because we never receive your data, we cannot access it, restore it, or produce it on request. If you want a copy, use the export button.

7. Legal basis and data-protection status

Under the GDPR, we are neither a controller nor a processor of the form data you save in Grispy, because that data is never transmitted to us and we never come into possession of it. It stays under your control on your own device throughout.

This policy covers the extension. If you later buy a licence, the payment is handled by a separate payment provider under its own privacy policy, and only your purchase details — not anything Grispy has stored — are involved. That will be named here when it applies.

8. Children

Grispy is a general-purpose tool and is not directed at children. It collects nothing from anyone, of any age.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the effective date above changes with it, and any change that narrows the protections described here will be announced in the extension's release notes before the version making the change is published.
