Grispy — Privacy Policy

Effective 9 September 2026. Applies to the Grispy browser extension for Chrome and Firefox.

Grispy sends nothing anywhere. It has no server, no account, no analytics and no tracking. Everything you save stays in your own browser, on your own computer, and can be encrypted with a passphrase only you know. We never receive it, so there is nothing for us to sell, lose or hand over.

1. What Grispy stores

When you save a form, Grispy records it on your device so it can fill the same form again later:

  • The form's structure — its field labels, names, types and the options in its dropdowns.
  • The address of each page the form spans (its origin and path; the query string and fragment are discarded).
  • The values you saved against that form, under whatever preset name you gave them.

Those values are whatever you chose to put in the form. That may include personal information — your name, address, telephone number, or anything else you typed. Grispy stores them as you typed them and does not treat one kind differently from another.

Grispy does work out what kind of detail a field is asking for — that this box wants an email address, that one a tax number — by reading the page's own field names, labels and autocomplete attributes. That is how it can fill a form it has never seen before. It happens entirely on your device and is never sent anywhere. If you correct it, the correction is saved against that form so the next visit is right.

Grispy also stores global presets: named sets of facts you reuse across different forms, rather than answers saved against one particular form. A global preset holds whatever you put in it, under a name you choose — typically things like a name, an address, a telephone number, a tax or social-security number, or a passport number.

A global preset may hold someone else's details rather than your own. That is what the feature is for: if you fill forms on behalf of clients, colleagues, applicants or customers, you can keep one preset for each of them and pick between them. Grispy knows what kind of detail each value is — that is how it fills a form it has never seen — and it has no idea whose details they are.

Where that data goes does not change — it stays on your device and is never transmitted, as sections 2 and 3 describe. What does change is who is holding it. If you store other people's personal information in Grispy, you are the one holding it, and the responsibility that comes with that is yours rather than ours. Section 7 sets out what that means.

Finally, Grispy stores the names of any detail types you add yourself. It ships knowing a fixed list — email, telephone, tax number and so on — and you can extend it: if you add "Blood type" as a detail, that name is recorded on your device so the same detail can be filled on other forms. The value itself is not stored with the name — it lives in whichever sets of details you put it in. One other thing can be recorded alongside the name: if you tell Grispy to recognise a detail on any form, it also stores the field name and the label that form used for it, exactly as that page spelled them, so it can recognise the same field elsewhere. It does not record which site you taught it on.

2. Where it is stored, and who can reach it

All of it is written to your browser's local extension storage (chrome.storage.local), which is a file inside your browser profile directory on your own computer. Grispy never uses your browser's sync storage, so nothing is copied to your other devices or to your browser vendor's servers.

Encryption, and why it is your choice

Grispy can encrypt what it saves — every value you saved, and the names you gave your forms and presets — with a passphrase only you know. The first time you save anything, Grispy asks whether you want one. You can decline, and it will not ask again; you can turn encryption on later from its options page whenever you change your mind.

When encryption is on, you enter your passphrase once per browser session. It never leaves your computer and is never sent anywhere. The key it produces is held in your browser's memory for that session alone — it is never written to disk — and it is gone once you close the browser.

Five limits you should know about, stated plainly:

  • If you decline the passphrase, nothing is encrypted. Your saved data is stored as ordinary readable text, and anything that can read files on your computer — other software running under your user account, another person on the same unlocked machine, or someone who takes an unencrypted laptop — can read it. Your operating system's full-disk encryption and a locked screen are then what protect it. This covers global presets as well as saved forms, which is worth weighing before you decline if yours will hold other people's identity details.
  • Encryption conceals what you saved, not which sites you saved it on. Grispy has to know whether a page has anything saved for it without asking for your passphrase on every page you visit — otherwise you would be prompted constantly, nearly always on pages with nothing saved. The list of addresses it holds data for is therefore left readable. Someone with access to the files on your computer could see which sites you have saved forms for, and how many forms and presets you have, without being able to read any of the values inside them.
  • A global preset's name is sealed; the fact that you have one is not. Global presets are encrypted like everything else you save, and they go one step further: there is no readable list of them anywhere, deliberately, because a plaintext index of other people's names is exactly the thing worth protecting. The name you gave a preset sits inside the encrypted record along with its contents. What someone with access to your files can still work out is how many global presets you have — not what any of them is called, and not whose details are in it. The names of any detail types you added yourself, and any field names and labels you taught them against, are sealed the same way, for the same reason: what you chose to keep track of can be as revealing as the value.
  • A forgotten passphrase cannot be recovered. Not by you, and not by us — we never have it. Your options at that point are an export you took while unlocked, or resetting Grispy, which deletes everything and starts you over. Resetting works even when you cannot unlock.
  • Uninstalling deletes it. Removing the extension removes its storage. Export your data first if you want to keep it.

3. What Grispy does not do

Sends your data to us or anyone elseNo. The extension makes no network requests of any kind.
Analytics, telemetry or crash reportingNone. We collect no usage data, not even anonymous counts.
Accounts or sign-inNone. Grispy has no user accounts.
Advertising, tracking or profilingNone, and no data is ever sold, rented or shared.
Third-party libraries or SDKsNone. No package is installed and no external code is loaded. The one piece of third-party material bundled is the path data that draws the icons, under open-source licences named in the extension's third-party notice; nothing is fetched at runtime.
Cookies, browsing history, downloadsNot read. Grispy holds no permission for any of them.
Reading pages in the backgroundNo. Grispy can only see a page after you click its toolbar button on that tab.

4. Permissions, and why each one exists

activeTabLets Grispy read the page in the one tab where you clicked its button, and only after you click. It expires. It grants no standing access to any site.
scriptingLets Grispy place its form-reading code into that same tab, at that same moment.
storageLets Grispy keep your saved forms on your device.

Grispy requests no host permissions. That is why installing it shows no "read and change your data on all websites" warning: it genuinely cannot, until you ask it to, on the one tab you asked it on.

5. How to check this for yourself

Three ways, none of which requires trusting this page:

  • Open your browser's extension settings and read Grispy's permission list. It is the three above. A permission the extension does not hold is a capability it does not have.
  • Unpack the extension and search its code for the ways a browser can reach the network — fetch, XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket, sendBeacon, EventSource. There are no matches, in any version we have published.
  • The encryption is your browser's own — the built-in WebCrypto implementation, AES-GCM for the saved data and PBKDF2 for the passphrase. Grispy ships no cryptographic code of its own, so there is no bespoke implementation of ours for you to have to trust.

6. Your control over your data

Grispy's options page lets you see everything it has stored, export it to a JSON file, and delete it — one preset, one form, or everything at once. When encryption is on you choose the export format: encrypted with your passphrase, or readable. A readable export is the only copy that survives a forgotten passphrase, so it is worth keeping one somewhere safe — and worth remembering that it is readable by anything that can open the file.

An export of everything includes your global presets, so if any of those hold other people's details, the file holds them too. Keep such a file where you would keep those details in any other form, and do not attach one to a bug report or email it to us. The support page asks the same thing; this is why it asks.

Deletion is immediate and final; there is no copy anywhere else to delete.

Because we never receive your data, we cannot access it, restore it, or produce it on request. If you want a copy, use the export button.

7. Legal basis and data-protection status

Under the GDPR, we are neither a controller nor a processor of the form data you save in Grispy, because that data is never transmitted to us and we never come into possession of it. It stays under your control on your own device throughout.

Your own position may not be the same as ours. If you use Grispy to hold other people's personal data — a client's, a colleague's, a customer's — then in relation to that data you are the one processing it, and where the GDPR applies to what you are doing, a controller's responsibilities are yours. We cannot carry them on your behalf, because the data never reaches us and we have no way to see, correct or delete it. Whether that applies to you is a question about your own activity rather than about Grispy, and it is worth putting to whoever advises you on such things.

This policy covers the extension. If you later buy a licence, the payment is handled by a separate payment provider under its own privacy policy, and only your purchase details — not anything Grispy has stored — are involved. That will be named here when it applies.

8. Children

Grispy is a general-purpose tool and is not directed at children. It collects nothing from anyone, of any age.
